# Foundation Training, 15 to 17 Oct 2026: trainer debrief

Aaditya's notes after the training, sorted. His words, grouped by topic.

## What the next training should focus on
Skills, practised with tools, more than theory:
- Sharing, bonding, relationship building
- Team-based thinking, topic-based thinking
- Observation
- Body language (Aaditya: "I need to work on body language")
- AIRC: Attention, Instruction, Information, Reason, Review, Confirmation
- Situational leadership

## What was covered
- Emergencies; how to identify risks and hazards; ways to rescue
- Maps: reading maps, making your own map, diagram and legend making. They barely understood contours.
- Hazards: natural and human, then an activity walking around to identify them. They need to learn how to give a report.
- Scenarios: rescue, Q scenarios, Leave No Trace scenarios
- Resource management intro (figuring out resources for camping); rescue management intro
- Observation practice; maps of Nepal; the Map of India game (call a city, they find it)
- Emergency observation: where a helicopter's noise comes from and where it goes, how long it takes to cross the area
- Three whistles: universal rescue signal
- One night camping: they chose the place, what to eat, the leader, and delegated. Cooked their own food, bird watching, lots of jugaad because items were forgotten, Leave No Trace in practice.
- Opening game: four village kids joined. Everyone paired up and learned about each other's life, three rounds, a handshake built with each person. Research (asking) and sharing are both skills; this builds relationships.

## What worked
- Skits and experiential learning helped a lot.
- Once Aaditya stepped out, they had no choice but to give a proper briefing and practise. Before, he was always there to butt in.
- They expected the training to be practical and were happy it was. They learned to make a campfire.

## Change next time
- More ways to visualise, less theory. Theory stays in the handbook; teaching is not a presentation on the wall.
- AIRC skits for each part: giving information, getting attention, asking a question, staying to the point. Topic does not have to be trekking (cricket, anything).
- Hazard walk followed by giving a report.
- Evening one-hour walks around sunset.
- More first aid through activities, each starting from one photo or image per topic (for example hypothermia and hyperthermia in one image).
- Waterfall safety by actually being near water, then an activity in the water.
- Break the group into team sections: documentation, technical, pantry, load ferry.
- Show real maps for practice; more games like Map of India.

## Trainer preparation
- Papers maintained and numbered, with a timetable of when to start what, and every activity written down.
- Practical activity cards in Hindi, English and Marathi that trainees can read and understand.
- All phones charged and ready (no network on site); one or two large battery packs.
